A becomes D

Selin thinks the scytale is too fiddly. Mia knows another method, which the Roman general Gaius Julius Caesar is said to have used: each letter is replaced by another one that comes a few places later in the alphabet. Mia uses 3 places: A becomes D.

On the page before you learnt about methods and keys. On this page you will encrypt and decrypt with the Caesar cipher.

The key is a number

In the Caesar cipher, the key is the number of places. After Z you start again at A: with the key 3, X becomes A, Y becomes B and Z becomes C. To decrypt, you go back the same number of places.

The school trip message, MEET ON MONDAY AT EIGHT AT THE STATION, with the key 3 is: PHHW RQ PRQGDB DW HLJKW DW WKH VWDWLRQ.

Remember

Encrypting means counting forwards, decrypting means counting backwards, always by the key. On the Caesar disc you read from the outside in when you encrypt, and from the inside out when you decrypt.

Try it out

On the outside of the disc is the plaintext, on the inside the ciphertext. With “+1” and “−1” you turn the inner ring, the number in the middle is the key. Turn it to the key of the task, read off each letter and type the word into the field. Then press “Check”.
