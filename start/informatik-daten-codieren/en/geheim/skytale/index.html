Class 6c must not read this!

Class 6b is planning a surprise for the other class, 6c, who are staying at the youth hostel too. Jona wants to write to Selin where they will meet. But class 6c must not be able to read the message, even if they find the note. Selin has an idea from her history book: a scytale, a wooden stick with a strip.

In the chapter “Everything is 0 and 1”, codes could be read by everyone. On this page you will learn how to encrypt a message so that only people who are in on it can read it.

Method and key

You can also do it with boxes instead of a stick, with the same idea: the letters are swapped around by a fixed rule. Jona writes MEET AT SIX without spaces in rows of 3 letters. Then he reads column by column from top to bottom.

encryption

When you encrypt, you turn the readable plaintext into a ciphertext with a method and a secret key. Only someone who knows the key is meant to be able to read it again.

key

The key is the secret you need to encrypt and decrypt, for example a number. Everyone may know the method, but not the key.

Remember

The method is the rule, here “write in rows, read in columns”. The key is the number you keep secret, here 3 letters per row. To decrypt, Selin writes the ciphertext column by column into boxes with 3 columns again and reads the rows.

Exercise

Selin gets the ciphertext BTTLOAWVATEE, 12 letters. The key is 3 letters per row. What is the plaintext?

  • BOATATTWELVE right answer

    12 letters in 3 columns make 4 rows. Selin writes BTTL, OAWV and ATEE as columns. The rows are BOA, TAT, TWE, LVE: boat at twelve.

  • BLWTTOVETAAE

    You encrypted the ciphertext again, by writing it in rows. To decrypt, you write it in columns.

    That is the ciphertext read backwards. The scytale mixes up the letters in a different way.
