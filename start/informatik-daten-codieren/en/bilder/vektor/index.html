A poster for the school trip

Jona and Selin are making a big poster for the school trip. It is going to have a red circle on it as the sun. They have stored the sun in two ways and enlarged both by the same amount.

On the page before you drew pictures with pixels. On this page you will learn a second way of storing pictures.

Exercise

Which sun was a bitmap image?

  • A right answer

    When you make it bigger, the pixels get bigger. You can see the single squares as steps.

  • B

    The edge of B is smooth. With a bitmap image the pixels would show as steps when made bigger.

A bitmap image only has as many pixels as are stored. If you make it bigger, each pixel becomes a big square. Some programs smooth the edge, then the picture becomes blurry instead of jagged.

Shapes instead of pixels

vector image

A vector image stores a picture as shapes such as circles, lines and rectangles, each with its numbers, for example the centre, the radius and the colour.

Sun B is a vector image. All that is stored is: a circle, centre (50 | 50), radius 40, colour red. (50 | 50) means 50 across and 50 up. The radius is the distance from the centre to the edge. When you make it bigger, the computer works out the circle again. That is why the edge stays smooth.

Bitmap and vector images compared
Comparisonbitmap imagevector image
storesevery pixelshapes with numbers
made biggerbecomes jagged or blurrystays smooth
good forphotoslogos and plans

Remember

Neither kind is always better. A photo has millions of pixels in many colours that you can hardly describe as shapes, so it is a bitmap image. A logo made of a few shapes should be sharp both big and small, so it is a vector image.
