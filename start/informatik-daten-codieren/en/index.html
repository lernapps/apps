Representing and encoding data

Class 6b is going on a school trip to Seeberg. Who is going, where, and when? You will write down this information again and again in new ways: as a table and a tree, as a number made of bits, as a pixel image and in secret writing, and one year later in class 7b as bytes and as a voice message. This is how you learn how a computer stores everything with 0 and 1. Chapters 1 to 4 for ages 10 to 12, chapter 5 for ages 12 to 14.

Start: Information and data

Contents

  1. Information becomes data

    A message becomes data, and data becomes information again, as a note, a table, a chart and a tree.

    Self-assessment

    1. Information and data Telling information and data apart: a note with data only becomes information when you know the context.
    2. Interpreting data Interpreting data in its context: is 21 an age, a temperature or a time?
    3. Table, chart, text Showing the same information as a tally, a table, a bar chart and a text, and choosing the form that fits.
    4. The tree The tree as a way of showing data: root, nodes, edges and leaves in the room plan and a tournament tree.
  2. Everything is 0 and 1

    Codes in everyday life, the bit cards, numbers in bits and how much a byte is.

    Self-assessment

    1. Codes in everyday life Recognising codes in everyday life, and encoding and decoding a message with Morse code.
    2. The bit cards The binary system with bit cards: read numbers made of 0 and 1 and turn them into decimal numbers.
    3. Numbers in bits Turning a decimal number into a binary number: with the bit cards and by dividing by 2 again and again.
    4. Bits and bytes Explaining the units bit, byte, kilobyte, megabyte and gigabyte, and comparing amounts of data.
  3. Pictures made of dots and shapes

    Encoding and decoding a picture as pixels, and comparing bitmap and vector images.

    Self-assessment

    1. Pictures made of pixels Storing a picture as a grid of pixels: black and white with 0 and 1, in colour with colour numbers.
    2. Pixels or vectors? Telling bitmap and vector images apart, and explaining which kind of image suits a purpose better.
  4. Secret messages

    Encrypting and decrypting a message with a scytale and a Caesar disc, and judging how secure a method is.

    Self-assessment

    1. Secret writing The basic idea of encryption with the scytale: plaintext, ciphertext, method and key.
    2. The Caesar disc Encrypting and decrypting with the Caesar cipher and a key, using a Caesar disc.
    3. How secure is it? Comparing encryption methods and judging how secure they are: how many keys would someone have to try?
  5. Encoding text and signals

    For ages 12 to 14. How characters become bytes with a code table, and how analog signals become digital data.

    Self-assessment

    1. Characters become numbers How letters are stored as binary numbers with a character set such as ASCII or Unicode.
    2. Analog and digital Explaining the difference between analog and digital and seeing how a voice is digitised.
