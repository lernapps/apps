  • Use the examples and tasks of this page. Do not make up your own. Work nothing out yourself: solutions and mistakes are below with each number.
  • In the chat you explain, with the page's example. Tasks are worked out in the app: the child solves them there. If it works a task out in the chat, send it the link to the task at once.

Context: the page “Codes in everyday life”

Recognising codes in everyday life, and encoding and decoding a message with Morse code.

Messages without words

Class 6b has arrived at Seeberg by train and is walking from the station to the youth hostel. Selin points at the street: there are messages without words everywhere, at the traffic lights, on the milk carton in Mia's bag, on the signs.

In the first chapter you saw how information becomes data. On this page you will learn about codes: fixed rules for turning data into other symbols.

Morse code

At Seeberg, Jona and Selin sleep in different rooms. In the evening they want to send each other messages with a torch. They practise on the way. For this they use Morse code: each letter is a series of short flashes (·) and long ones (–). Between two letters they pause, shown here as a gap.

Part of the Morse code table:

  • Letter | Signal

  • A | · –

  • E | ·

  • I | · ·

  • M | – –

  • N | – ·

  • O | – – –

  • S | · · ·

  • T | –

  • code: A code is a fixed rule that turns symbols into other symbols. To encode means to turn a message into the code. To decode means to turn it back.

Remember: Remember

The sender and the receiver must know the same code table. And the pauses are part of the code. Without a pause, “· · ·” (S) might become “· ·” and “·” (I and E).

Exercise zeichen-bedeutung

  • Task: What does the sign mean? Match them.
  • Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
  • Solution: The red man at the crossing lights up. – Stop and wait.; The green man at the crossing lights up. – You may cross.; The till beeps at the barcode on the milk. – The till has read the number of the product.
  • Explanation: Each sign stands for a meaning by a fixed rule. If you know the rule, you understand the sign without words.

Exercise morse-decodieren

  • Task: Selin flashes: · · · | · | · . The lines | are pauses. Which word is it?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ SEE – · · · is S, then · twice for E: SEE, as in Seeberg (and the English word see).
    • ✗ EEEEE – That is how you read it if you ignore the pauses. The first three dots belong to one letter.
    • ✗ OEE – O is three long flashes, – – –. But Selin flashes three short ones.
  • Solution: SEE
  • Explanation: With the pauses, every letter is clear. Without pauses you could read the signals in a very different way.
  • Link: ../?via=tutor#morse-decodieren

Exercise nest-codieren

  • Task: Jona wants to send NEST in Morse code. Put the signals in the right order.
  • Answer format: put the items in the right order (the app shows them shuffled)
  • Solution: 1. – ·, 2. ·, 3. · · ·, 4. –
  • Explanation: N is – ·, E is ·, S is · · ·, T is –. The order of the letters decides the order of the signals.
  • Link: ../?via=tutor#nest-codieren

Exercise sos-zeichen

  • Task: How many flashes does Jona need for SOS? Count every dot and every dash.
  • Answer format: type a number
  • Solution: 9
  • Explanation: S is · · ·, O is – – –, S is · · ·. That is 3 + 3 + 3 = 9 signals.
  • Typical mistakes and what the app says:
    • 3: 3 is the number of letters. But each letter has several signals.
    • 6: Look at O in the table again. O has three long signals.
  • Link: ../?via=tutor#sos-zeichen

Exercise code-geheim

  • Task: A code is always secret.
  • Answer format: choose “true” or “false”
  • Solution: The statement is false.
  • Explanation: Anyone can know Morse code, the traffic lights and the barcode. A code is mainly there to help pass on messages. You will learn how to keep a message secret in the chapter “Secret messages”.
