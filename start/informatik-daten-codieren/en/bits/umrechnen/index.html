Bus number 27

On Tuesday, class 6b goes on an outing on bus number 27. Jona wants to put the number on the room door with bit cards, so that everyone knows. First he practises with other numbers: make the number the task gives. The 27 comes after that.

On the page before you read bit cards and turned them into a number. Now you go the other way: a number becomes bits.

Solution

Two methods to get the binary number

Method 1: The biggest card first. Go from left to right. For each card, ask: does it still fit into what is left? Then turn it over and take its value away.

Method 1 for the number 27
CardDoes it fit?Still to make
16yes: 127 − 16 = 11
8yes: 111 − 8 = 3
4no: 03
2yes: 13 − 2 = 1
1yes: 11 − 1 = 0

Method 2: Keep dividing by 2. Divide the number by 2 and write down the remainder. Divide the result by 2 again, until you get 0. The remainders, read from the bottom up, are the binary number. This method also works for big numbers, without cards.

Method 2 for the number 27

Read the remainders from the bottom up: 11011. Check: 16 + 8 + 2 + 1 = 27.
DivisionRemainder
27 ÷ 2 = 131
13 ÷ 2 = 61
6 ÷ 2 = 30
3 ÷ 2 = 11
1 ÷ 2 = 01

Remember

When you divide, the remainder for card 1 comes out first. That is why you read the remainders from the bottom up. If you read them from the top, the number is the wrong way round.

Practice

Exercise

Three worked tasks, each with its solution:

  1. Task no. 1

    Write the number 14 as a binary number. You may leave out zeros at the front.

    Hint

    Keep dividing by 2 and write down each remainder. Or ask for each card from 16 down: does it still fit?

    Solution
    1. Dividing: 14 ÷ 2 = 7 remainder 0; 7 ÷ 2 = 3 remainder 1; 3 ÷ 2 = 1 remainder 1; 1 ÷ 2 = 0 remainder 1.
    2. The remainders from the bottom up: 1110. Check with the cards: 8 + 4 + 2 = 14.

    Right answer: 1,110

  2. Task no. 2

    Write the number 4 as a binary number. You may leave out zeros at the front.

    Hint

    Keep dividing by 2 and write down each remainder. Or ask for each card from 16 down: does it still fit?

    Solution
    1. Dividing: 4 ÷ 2 = 2 remainder 0; 2 ÷ 2 = 1 remainder 0; 1 ÷ 2 = 0 remainder 1.
    2. The remainders from the bottom up: 100. Check with the cards: 4 = 4.

    Right answer: 100

  3. Task no. 3

    Write the number 29 as a binary number. You may leave out zeros at the front.

    Hint

    Keep dividing by 2 and write down each remainder. Or ask for each card from 16 down: does it still fit?

    Solution
    1. Dividing: 29 ÷ 2 = 14 remainder 1; 14 ÷ 2 = 7 remainder 0; 7 ÷ 2 = 3 remainder 1; 3 ÷ 2 = 1 remainder 1; 1 ÷ 2 = 0 remainder 1.
    2. The remainders from the bottom up: 11101. Check with the cards: 16 + 8 + 4 + 1 = 29.

    Right answer: 11,101

Exercise

Selin turns 13 into a binary number. Put her steps in the right order.

Give each entry its place: 1 for the first.

Right answer: 1. 13 ÷ 2 = 6 remainder 1, 2. 6 ÷ 2 = 3 remainder 0, 3. 3 ÷ 2 = 1 remainder 1, 4. 1 ÷ 2 = 0 remainder 1

Each step divides the result of the step before. Read from the bottom, the remainders are 1101, which is 13.

Exercise

Mia writes the number 40 as a binary number. How many digits (bits) does it have?

Right answer: 6

40 = 32 + 8. The row goes from 32 down to 1, which is 6 digits: 101000.

Exercise

Which binary number is the number 12?

  • 1100 right answer

    8 + 4 = 12. Cards 8 and 4 are face up, 2 and 1 face down.

  • 0011

    That is 1100 the wrong way round. 0011 is 2 + 1 = 3.

  • 1010

    1010 is 8 + 2 = 10.

12 = 8 + 4, so cards 8 and 4 are face up: 1100.
