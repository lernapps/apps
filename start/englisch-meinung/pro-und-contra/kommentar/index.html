Zwei Schalen und eine Frage

In einem Kommentar (einem argumentativen Text) wägst du eine Frage ab, zu der es mehr als eine Meinung gibt. Diese Frage kennen viele aus dem Schulalltag. Pro heißt hier: für ein Verbot. Contra heißt: gegen ein Verbot. Im Lesestück belegst du mit den Absatznummern.

Die Zahlen sind die Absätze. Alle Zahlen im Text stammen aus den Quellen unter dem Text. Achtung: Es gibt zwei verschiedene Angaben mit 58 Prozent. Absatz 1 zählt Länder, Absatz 2 zählt Schülerinnen und Schüler.

Phones at school: should schools ban them?

1 Should schools ban phones during the school day? More and more countries have decided to limit phones in schools. An overview on Wikipedia, which refers to UNESCO's monitoring, says that the share of countries with a ban rose from 23 per cent in 2023 to 40 per cent in 2024 and to 58 per cent in 2026. In England, government guidance asks schools to be phone-free by default, even at break and lunchtime. This comment weighs the arguments for a ban (Pro) and against a ban (Contra) and then reaches a conclusion.

2 On the one hand, there are strong reasons to keep phones out of lessons. First of all, phones can distract. In England's National Behaviour Survey for 2024 to 2025, 58 per cent of secondary pupils reported that phones were used without permission in at least some lessons. The government says that this distracts not only the pupil with the phone but the whole class. The survey counts use without permission, so it does not show how much learning is lost.

3 Moreover, a phone-free break leaves more room for other things. The government guidance points out that screen time can take the place of talking to friends, exercise and sleep. Without phones, a break can become a time to meet and to move.

4 A third argument is about unkind messages. According to Ofcom data quoted by the government, 31 per cent of children aged 8 to 17 have experienced someone being nasty or hurtful towards them through communications technology. That is not only a school problem, but supporters of bans hope that fewer such messages are sent during the school day.

5 On the other hand, there are also good arguments against a strict ban. Phones help with safety. Pupils can call home if a bus is late or plans change, and many families like to be able to reach their children. Schools can solve a part of this problem. In England, parents can contact the school office in an emergency, and some schools let pupils hand their phones to staff in the morning and collect them at the end of the day.

6 In addition, supporters of phones say that they can be a tool for learning. In a study from 2017 with about a hundred students at a British university, 45 per cent said that phones in class support their education. The most common example was digital access to textbooks. The researcher added that some answers may only show what students think they should say. The group was small and older than school pupils, so the result is only a hint.

7 However, a ban may achieve less than some people hope. A study published in 2026 in the Quarterly Journal of Economics looked at lockable phone pouches in schools. It found only limited effects on well-being and on learning results. The study is about pouches, not about every kind of ban.

8 To sum up, both sides have real arguments. Distraction in lessons is a clear concern, because many pupils report phones being used without permission. Safety and learning matter too, but schools can often deal with them, for example with a phone drop-off or a call through the office. At the same time, the research on bans is limited, so the benefits should not be overstated. Therefore, schools should keep phones away during lessons. For breaks the arguments are closer, so each school should decide carefully, as long as pupils can still be reached in an emergency.

Quellen, abgerufen am 24.11.2026: gov.uk, „Mobile phones in schools“ und „Communicating your policy for prohibiting the use of mobile phones in schools to parents“ (Stand 29.06.2026); Wikipedia, „Mobile phone use in schools“ (Studie von 2026 im Quarterly Journal of Economics zu Sperrbeuteln; Studie von 2017 mit etwa 100 Studierenden). Die Waage und der Aufbau

Merke

Die Waage: Jede Schale der Waage besteht aus Burgern: Opinion, Reason, Example. Das Example ist der Beleg, und ein Argument mit Beleg wiegt mehr. Pro liegt in der roten Schale, Contra in der blauen. Bei der Frage „Should schools ban phones?“ ist Pro ein Argument für das Verbot, Contra ein Argument dagegen. Die Neigung der Waage ist die conclusion, die Schlussfolgerung.

Der Aufbau: Die Einleitung nennt die Frage und kündigt beide Seiten an. Im Hauptteil hat jeder Absatz einen Gedanken. Erst kommen alle Pro-Absätze, dann alle Contra-Absätze, und jedes Argument hat einen Beleg (Zahl, Quelle oder Beispiel). Der Schluss entscheidet sich und bringt kein neues Argument. Mehr zum Gliedern von Texten: Texte gliedern.

Verbindungswörter: On the one hand … On the other hand … (zwei Seiten) · First of all, Moreover, In addition, (weiteres Argument) · However, (Gegensatz) · Therefore, (Folge) · To sum up, In conclusion, (Schluss). Beispiel: The bag is cheap. However, it breaks easily.

Ton: Ein Kommentar ist sachlich. Statt I think … passt oft besser In my opinion, … oder The arguments for … are stronger.

Aufgabe

Wähle für jede Lücke das Verbindungswort, das zum Gedanken passt.

  1. Eine Reise ist spannend. Sie kostet aber Geld. → A trip is exciting. …, it costs money.
  2. Der Bus kommt oft zu spät. Deshalb fahren viele mit dem Rad. → The bus is often late. …, many pupils go by bike.
  3. Radfahren ist gesund. Außerdem ist es billig. → Cycling is healthy. …, it is cheap.
  4. Einerseits ist Reiten schön. Andererseits ist es teuer. → On the one hand, riding is fun. …, it is expensive.
Lösung
  1. A trip is exciting. However, it costs money.
  2. The bus is often late. Therefore, many pupils go by bike.
  3. Cycling is healthy. Moreover, it is cheap.
  4. On the one hand, riding is fun. On the other hand, it is expensive.

Offene Aufgabe

Wähle eine Frage, die Jugendliche beschäftigt, zum Beispiel „Should schools sell sweets?“. Schreibe 100 bis 150 Wörter mit Einleitung, Pro-Absatz, Contra-Absatz und Schluss.

Tipp

Die App bewertet nicht. Prüfe deinen Text anhand der Punkte.

Eine gute Antwort enthält:

  • Die Einleitung nennt die Frage und sagt, dass Pro und Contra kommen. Nachlesen
  • Pro und Contra stehen in getrennten Absätzen, mit einem Gedanken je Absatz. Nachlesen
  • Jedes Argument hat einen Beleg, zum Beispiel eine Zahl oder ein Beispiel. Nachlesen
  • Der Schluss entscheidet sich mit therefore oder to sum up und bringt kein neues Argument. Nachlesen
  • Der Text ist sachlich und nutzt Verbindungswörter wie however und moreover. (Form)
Lösung

Should every pupil learn to cook at school?

Some schools offer cooking lessons, while others use the time for other subjects. This comment weighs both sides.

On the one hand, cooking is a useful skill for life. For example, a pupil who can make a simple meal can look after himself or herself later. Moreover, cooking in a group builds teamwork.

On the other hand, cooking lessons need a kitchen, equipment and money. For example, a school without a kitchen would have to build one first. However, this problem can be solved step by step.

To sum up, both sides have strong arguments, but the skill is more important than the cost. Therefore, every pupil should learn to cook, even if a school needs time to build a kitchen.
