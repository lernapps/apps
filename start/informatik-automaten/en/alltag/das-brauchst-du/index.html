Can you do this already? “I can explain the IPO model (input, process, output) and sort devices and steps into its three stages.” Class 6b is planning a juice machine made from a cardboard box for their school fair. Jona, Selin and Mia are all eleven. Jona wants to sit in the box and be the machine, Mia wants to buy, and Selin writes everything down. A juice is going to cost €2. This is how the machine looks in the IPO picture:

IInputput in a coin, press the button
PProcessJona checks: is there enough money?
⇅storage: keeps thingsHow much money is inside already?
OOutputjuice drops out

The IPO picture of the juice machine: three boxes with arrows. Box I “Input” (blue): put in a coin, press the button. Box P “Process” (green): Jona checks whether there is enough money. Box O “Output” (orange): juice drops out. A dashed drawer is attached to the box Process: “storage: keeps things”, with the question “How much money is inside already?”.

The IPO picture shows the way from input to output. But it does not tell you whether a juice really comes out when you press the button. For that, Jona has to know what has happened before: how much money is inside already. The IPO picture only has the box “storage” for that. It does not show how what is kept changes with each input. The next pages are about this memory.

Exercise

Mia presses the button. What does the IPO picture tell you about whether a juice comes out?

  • It depends on the money already inside. right answer

    The IPO picture only shows the way. Whether juice comes out depends on what was put in before.

  • A juice always comes out.

    Without enough money the machine gives no juice, even when the button is pressed.

  • A juice never comes out.

    When there is enough money inside, the button brings a juice.

The same button can bring a juice one time and nothing another time. What makes the difference is on the next page.
