Mia's first juice

It is class 6b's school fair. The class has built a juice machine out of a cardboard box. A juice costs €2, and the machine only takes €1 coins. Jona sits in the box and is the machine. Selin writes down what happens. Mia puts in a coin and presses the button. No juice comes out. On this page you find out why a machine reacts to the same button in one way at one time and in another way at another time.

A cardboard juice machine with Jona's note. At the start it says “€0 paid”. What happens:

  • Coin at €0 or €1: Jona keeps it and remembers €1 more.
  • Coin at €2: the coin comes back.
  • Button at €0 or €1: nothing happens.
  • Button at €2: a juice drops out, and afterwards Jona remembers “€0 paid”.

With JavaScript you press “coin” and “button” yourself.

What the machine remembers

The same button twice: once nothing comes out, once a juice. The difference is on Jona's note. The IPO picture shows what goes in and what comes out. If you look at the device as an automaton (a machine that remembers), what it remembers right now counts too. That is called its state. An input can change the state: after a coin, the note says €1 more.

automaton

An automaton (plural automata) reacts to inputs and remembers something as it does. What it does depends on the input and on what it has remembered.

state

The state is what a machine remembers right now, for example “€1 paid”.

Remember

What a machine does depends on two things, the input and its state. In the same state it always reacts to the same input in the same way.

Exercise

Someone says: “A machine always reacts to the same input in the same way, whatever it has remembered.” Is that true?

Right answer: The statement is false.

In the juice machine the button gives a juice at €2 and nothing at €1. The reaction depends on the input and on the state. Only in the same state is it always the same.

Play the machine

Play the juice machine in pairs, without a computer:

  1. One person is the machine. They have a note that says “€0 paid”.
  2. The other person buys. They say “coin” or “button”.
  3. The machine looks at its note, says what comes out and writes down the new amount.
  4. Swap after five inputs. Who makes a mistake?

Exercise

Other machines in everyday life remember something too. What does each machine remember?

Choose the match for each entry.

Right answer: pedestrian crossing with a button – who has green and whether someone has pressed; lift – the floor where you can get in right now; turnstile at the swimming pool – whether someone has paid; lamp with a push button – whether the light is on

Each of these machines reacts to the same input in different ways, depending on what it remembers. The button of the lamp switches it on when it is off, and off when it is on. You cannot see everything at the crossing: the lights remember that someone has pressed, even while the cars still have green.

Exercise

Selin writes cards for the game: “put in a coin”, “€1 paid”, “press the button”, “juice drops out”. Which card names a state?

  • “€1 paid” right answer

    Jona remembers this on his note. It stays like this until the next input comes.

  • “put in a coin”

    That is an input. It happens in a moment and can change the state.

  • “press the button”

    That is an input, like the coin. What happens then depends on the state.

  • “juice drops out”

    That is an output. It comes out when the machine reacts. The machine does not remember it.

A state is what the machine remembers. Inputs and outputs happen in a moment.
