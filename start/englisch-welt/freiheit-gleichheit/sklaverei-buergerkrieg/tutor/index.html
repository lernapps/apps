Lesestück: Slavery and the Civil War

  1. Slavery was legal in the United States from 1776 until 1865, mostly in the South. An enslaved person was treated as property: others could buy, sell or give away that person, and children were born into slavery.

  2. By 1805 every Northern state had begun to end slavery, some of them only slowly. In the South it grew. After the cotton gin was invented, farms in the Deep South needed more and more workers, and the demand for enslaved people rose.

  3. Congress banned the import of enslaved people from abroad in 1808. But more than a million people were sold inside the country, from the Upper South to the Deep South. By the census of 1860, about four million people were enslaved.

  4. In the 1850s the biggest political fight was about the West: should slavery spread into the new territories? Many Southern leaders said yes. Many people in the North wanted to stop it.

  5. In 1860 Abraham Lincoln won the presidential election. He belonged to the Republican Party and opposed the spread of slavery into the West. Seven Southern states left the Union. Later four more followed, and the eleven states together formed the Confederacy.

  6. The war began on 12 April 1861, when Confederate forces fired on Fort Sumter, a fort in the harbour of Charleston, South Carolina.

  7. Historians today agree that slavery was at the centre of the Southern states' decision. For the North, the main aim at the start was to keep the Union together: it refused to accept that states could simply leave. So the two sides had different aims, and historians still discuss the reasons of the Union in detail.

  8. On 1 January 1863, Lincoln signed the Emancipation Proclamation. It declared people who were enslaved in the states in rebellion to be free, and it allowed Black men to join the Union army. It applied to more than three and a half million of the four million enslaved people, but it could only be enforced where the Union army advanced.

  9. Enslaved people did not wait for this decree. Many had already escaped and looked for freedom behind Union lines.

  10. In the summer of 1863 the Union won at Vicksburg, which split the Confederacy in two at the Mississippi River. At Gettysburg, General Lee's attack on the North failed.

  11. In 1864 Lincoln put General Grant in command of all the Union armies. On 9 April 1865, after the Confederates had left their capital, Richmond, General Lee surrendered to General Grant at Appomattox. An estimated 700,000 soldiers had died. In December 1865 the Thirteenth Amendment to the Constitution ended slavery in the whole country, except as a punishment for a crime.

  12. Then began the time of Reconstruction: an attempt to rebuild the South and to give civil rights to the four million people who had been freed.
