Lesestück: Counting migration

  1. Migration means that people move from one country to another to live there. They do so for many reasons: to work, to join family, to study or to find safety. Only some of them are refugees.
  2. Two numbers look alike. In the censuses of 2021 and 2022, 10.7 million residents of the United Kingdom were born abroad: 16 per cent of the population. In the United States, 53.3 million residents were born abroad as of January 2025: 15.8 per cent.
  3. But the numbers come from different years and different counts. A census asks all households. It shows where people were born, but it gives no information about their immigration status or how long they plan to stay.
  4. Yearly estimates answer other questions. According to the Office for National Statistics, in the year to June 2025 about 898,000 people came to live in the United Kingdom and 693,000 left. The net migration, the difference, was plus 204,000. This is a provisional estimate.
  1. The reasons for migration also change over time. After 1948, many people came to Britain from countries of the former British Empire, among them passengers on the ship Empire Windrush. After 2004, many came from Central and Eastern Europe. Free movement for citizens of the European Union ended on 31 December 2020. In 2022 the United Kingdom created special visas for people from Ukraine, and by 31 March 2025, 223,000 people had arrived this way.
  2. In 2016 the United States admitted 1.18 million legal immigrants. Of these, 48 per cent were close relatives of American citizens, 12 per cent came for work and 13 per cent were refugees or asylum seekers.
  3. A minority is a group that is smaller than the rest of the population. It does not mean that people came from abroad. In 2024 an estimated 3.43 million residents identified as American Indian or Alaska Native alone, about 1 per cent of the population. This is the name the census uses for people from the first nations of the continent. You know them as Native Americans from chapter 3. Their ancestors lived on the continent long before the United States existed. The federal government recognises 574 tribes.
  4. To compare two countries, you need the same year, the same definition and the same kind of source. These figures come from three articles of the English Wikipedia (see the source of the figure below), which name the census and the statistics offices as their sources.

Offene Aufgabe

Quellen vergleichen: Zeile 2 nennt 16 Prozent für Großbritannien und 15,8 Prozent für die USA. Haben beide Länder gleich viele im Ausland Geborene? Schreibe drei Sätze auf Englisch und nenne, was du vor einem Vergleich prüfst.

Tipp

Schau in die Zeilen 2, 3 und 8.

Eine gute Antwort enthält:

  • Du sagst, dass die Anteile ähnlich sind (16 und 15,8 Prozent) und die absoluten Zahlen sehr verschieden (10,7 und 53,3 Millionen). Nachlesen
  • Du nennst, was du prüfen würdest, zum Beispiel das Jahr (2021 und 2022, Januar 2025), die Zählweise und die Quelle. Nachlesen
  • Du schreibst ganze Sätze und trennst Fakten von deiner Meinung. (Form)
Lösung

The shares are similar: 16 per cent in the United Kingdom and 15.8 per cent in the United States. But the numbers come from different years, 2021 and 2022 and January 2025. Before I compare them, I would check the year, the definition of born abroad and the original source.

Zahlen vergleichen

Merke

Vergleiche Zahlen nur, wenn Jahr, Definition und Art der Quelle zusammenpassen. Nenne bei jeder Zahl das Jahr und die Quelle: According to the census, 16 per cent of the residents were born abroad. Born abroad (im Ausland geboren) und minority (Minderheit) bedeuten nicht dasselbe.

Aufgabe

Ergänze das Wort.

Tipp

Drei Wörter für Menschen, die wandern.

  1. Eine Minderheit ist eine kleinere Gruppe in der Bevölkerung. → A … is a smaller group in the population.
  2. Menschen, die in ein Land einwandern, nennt man Einwanderer. → People who move into a country are called ….
  3. Wer flieht, um Schutz zu finden, ist ein Flüchtling. → A person who flees to find safety is a ….
Lösung
  1. A minority is a smaller group in the population.
  2. People who move into a country are called immigrants.
  3. A person who flees to find safety is a refugee.
