Case 1: Three photos of the lake

On the last page you learned: a photo is protected. It belongs to the person who took it, its creator. They decides what may happen to it. The picture search shows Selin three photos of the lake. Each one has a different note under it.

On this page you learn when you may use someone else's picture and how to give a credit.

When you may use someone else's picture

You may use someone else's picture if its creator allows you to. There are two ways:

  • You ask the creator and she says yes. Ask an adult to help you write to her.
  • She has already allowed everyone, with a free licence.
free licence

A permission for everyone that the creator adds to her work. Well-known ones are the Creative Commons licences, CC for short. The number after it, such as 4.0, is the version of the licence.

Two free licences and what you must do then
LicenceYou must
CC BYname the creator and the licence with a link (on paper, the web address of the licence) and say whether you changed anything
CC0nothing: the work is free for everyone. Giving the name is still fair.
credit

A short text next to the picture: title, creator, source (the website the picture is on, best with its web address) and licence.

In class or online?

In class, and to show at your school, you may use someone else's picture for your lessons. A law allows this, the German Copyright Act. It also says you must give a credit with the creator's name.

Online, where everyone can see it, also on the school website, you need permission or a free licence. If you are not sure, ask your teacher.

The judgement scale

At the end of each case you make a judgement: is it okay? For this there is a scale with five steps, from 1 “not okay” to 5 “okay”. First you check each reason: does it count for, against or not at all? A reason can count and still weigh little: a wish weighs less than a law. Then you choose the step that fits the reasons. Often more than one step fits.

The judgement scale has five steps: 1 not okay, 2 mostly not, 3 it depends, 4 mostly okay, 5 okay. Example: Selin puts photo 2 with a credit on the poster in the classroom. Judgement: step 5. Reasons for: the CC BY licence allows it. Selin names the creator and the licence. Reasons against: none. Does not count: “Everyone does it like this.”
