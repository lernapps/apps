## Rules of this app

- Explain current, voltage and resistance with the water picture (pump, pipe) and say that every picture has limits. Electrons and atoms do not appear in this app.
- The app is about direct current, as batteries supply it. Alternating current comes later. Talk about the socket only as a warning: never experiment with it.
- Calculations on paper, the result in the app. Watch the units: amperes (not milliamperes), volts, ohms.
- For building tasks and measuring exercises, any build that does what the task says counts, not only the model solution. Let the learners give reasons instead of guessing.

## Context: the page “Staying safe with electricity”

Sockets, batteries and thunderstorms. You learn what is dangerous and what is not.

### The idea

Electricity is useful but can be dangerous. The battery in your torch is harmless. The current from a
socket, on the other hand, can **injure you fatally**. If you know the rules, you stay safe.

### What to remember

- **Fuse**: A fuse breaks the circuit automatically when too much current flows, for example in a short circuit. This protects against fires.

You should know these rules:
- **Never** put objects into a socket, not even needles or forks.
- **Never** touch electrical devices **with wet hands**, and do not use them in the bathtub.
- Pull on the **plug**, not on the cable, and do not use devices with a damaged cable.
- **Never experiment with current from the socket.** For experiments use batteries only.
- **In a thunderstorm** stay indoors or in a car. In an open field and under lone trees you are in danger.

**Remember: Battery and socket**

A flat battery has 4.5 volts and is harmless to you. The socket supplies 230 volts. That is far too much
for your body.

### Example

**Example: A hairdryer falls into the bathtub**

Water conducts electricity, and so does your body. That is why no electrical device may come near bath
water. Never bathe with a device plugged in, and do not put devices on the edge of the tub.

### Exercise si-sicher

- Task: What is safe?
- Answer format: choose all right answers
- Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
  - ✓ Making a small bulb shine with a torch battery. – A small battery has only a few volts and is harmless to you.
  - ✓ Pulling the plug by the plug, not by the cable. – The cable can be damaged when you pull it. Pulling on the plug is right.
  - ✗ Poking a fork into the socket. – The socket has 230 volts. That can injure you fatally.
  - ✗ Touching a hairdryer with wet hands. – Wet skin conducts electricity well. That makes an electric shock very dangerous.
- Solution: Making a small bulb shine with a torch battery.; Pulling the plug by the plug, not by the cable.
- Explanation: Batteries with a few volts are harmless. For the socket with 230 volts strict rules apply.

### Exercise si-gewitter

- Task: A thunderstorm is coming and you are in a meadow. What do you do?
- Answer format: choose one answer
- Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
  - ✓ I go into a building or into a car. – In buildings and cars you are well protected from lightning.
  - ✗ I stand under a single big tree. – Lone tall trees are often struck by lightning. That is dangerous.
  - ✗ I stay in the middle of the meadow and stand upright. – In an open field you are the highest point, and lightning can strike you.
- Solution: I go into a building or into a car.
- Explanation: In a thunderstorm you look for a safe place such as a building or a car and avoid open areas and lone trees.

### Exercise si-sicherung

- Task: A fuse breaks the circuit when too much current flows.
- Answer format: choose “true” or “false”
- Solution: The statement is true.
- Explanation: The fuse switches off in an overload or a short circuit. This stops cables from getting too hot.
