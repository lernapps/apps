The idea

Electricity is useful but can be dangerous. The battery in your torch is harmless. The current from a socket, on the other hand, can injure you fatally. If you know the rules, you stay safe.

What to remember

Fuse

A fuse breaks the circuit automatically when too much current flows, for example in a short circuit. This protects against fires.

You should know these rules:

  • Never put objects into a socket, not even needles or forks.
  • Never touch electrical devices with wet hands, and do not use them in the bathtub.
  • Pull on the plug, not on the cable, and do not use devices with a damaged cable.
  • Never experiment with current from the socket. For experiments use batteries only.
  • In a thunderstorm stay indoors or in a car. In an open field and under lone trees you are in danger.

Battery and socket

A flat battery has 4.5 volts and is harmless to you. The socket supplies 230 volts. That is far too much for your body.

Example

A hairdryer falls into the bathtub

Water conducts electricity, and so does your body. That is why no electrical device may come near bath water. Never bathe with a device plugged in, and do not put devices on the edge of the tub.

Exercise

What is safe?

  • Making a small bulb shine with a torch battery. right answer

    A small battery has only a few volts and is harmless to you.

  • Pulling the plug by the plug, not by the cable. right answer

    The cable can be damaged when you pull it. Pulling on the plug is right.

  • Poking a fork into the socket.

    The socket has 230 volts. That can injure you fatally.

  • Touching a hairdryer with wet hands.

    Wet skin conducts electricity well. That makes an electric shock very dangerous.

Batteries with a few volts are harmless. For the socket with 230 volts strict rules apply.

Exercise

A thunderstorm is coming and you are in a meadow. What do you do?

  • I go into a building or into a car. right answer

    In buildings and cars you are well protected from lightning.

  • I stand under a single big tree.

    Lone tall trees are often struck by lightning. That is dangerous.

  • I stay in the middle of the meadow and stand upright.

    In an open field you are the highest point, and lightning can strike you.

In a thunderstorm you look for a safe place such as a building or a car and avoid open areas and lone trees.
