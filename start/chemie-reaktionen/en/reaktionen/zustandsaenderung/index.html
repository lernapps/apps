Why

When ice melts, it is still water. When wood burns, no wood is left. Both change a substance, but in very different ways. On this page you learn to tell them apart.

Rule

Change of state

In a change of state such as melting or evaporating the substance stays the same. Only the state of matter changes.

Ask yourself: is the substance still the same afterwards? If so, it is a change of state. You can usually reverse it: water vapour becomes water again when it cools. If a new substance was formed, it was a chemical reaction.

Example

A candle burns

Both happen at a burning candle. The wax below the flame melts and becomes liquid: that is a change of state, it stays wax. The evaporated wax burns in the flame to other substances, for example carbon dioxide and water vapour: that is a chemical reaction.

Practice

Exercise

Which of these processes are chemical reactions?

  • Wood burns. right answer

    Burning forms new substances, for example carbon dioxide and ash. It is a chemical reaction.

  • Hydrogen burns to water. right answer

    Hydrogen and oxygen become water, a new substance. It is a chemical reaction.

  • Ice melts.

    Melted ice is still water. Only the state changes.

  • Water evaporates.

    Water vapour is still water. It is only a change of state.

When melting and evaporating the substance stays the same, only the state changes. When burning, new substances are formed.

Exercise

Candle wax is warmed in a pot and becomes liquid. Which statement is right?

  • It is a change of state; the wax stays wax. right answer

    When it cools it becomes solid again. The substance has stayed the same.

  • It is a chemical reaction because the wax changes.

    Only the state has changed. No new substance was formed.

  • It is a chemical reaction because heat is supplied.

    Heat alone does not show a reaction. What counts is whether a new substance is formed.

When melting, the wax stays the same; only the state changes.

Exercise

When sugar is heated strongly, it turns brown and smells of caramel. That is a change of state.

Right answer: The statement is false.

The brown substance has different properties from sugar: colour and smell are new, and it does not turn back into sugar when it cools. It is a chemical reaction.
