Recap

This page goes over something you should already know. You can practise it in other apps: building block on the learning map of lernapps.net

Rule

Three properties help to sort substances:

  • The state of matter at room temperature (20 °C): solid, liquid or gaseous.
  • The melting point: from this temperature a solid becomes liquid.
  • The boiling point: from this temperature a liquid becomes gaseous.
Melting point

The temperature at which a solid becomes liquid. For a pure substance it is always the same.

Boiling point

The temperature at which a liquid becomes gaseous. For a pure substance it is always the same.

Memory aid

If room temperature (20 °C) is below the melting point, the substance is solid. If it lies between the melting and the boiling point, it is liquid. If it is above, it is gaseous.

Example

Why is water liquid at 20 °C?

Water melts at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C. Room temperature (20 °C) lies in between. That is why water is liquid at 20 °C.
