Chemistry – chemical reactions

When is something a chemical reaction, and how do you write it down? You recognise reactions, tell them from changes of state, explain conservation of mass with a balance and write simple reaction equations. Before that you briefly recap what you need for it. You practise on every page. For the lower grades (grades 7 and 8).

Contents

  1. Prior knowledge, recapped

    What you need to know before the reactions: properties of substances, states of matter, the particle model, symbols and formulas.

    1. Properties of substances – a short recap (recap) Colour, smell, hardness, density: this is how you recognise a substance. This is how you describe it.
    2. States of matter – a short recap (recap) Solid, liquid, gaseous: this is how you tell whether only the state changes in a process.
    3. Particle model – a short recap (recap) Substances consist of tiny particles. You need this to understand what happens to the atoms in a reaction.
    4. Symbols and formulas – a short recap (recap) Every element has a symbol, every compound a formula. This is how you read them before you write reaction equations.
  2. Chemical reactions

    You recognise chemical reactions, tell them from changes of state, explain conservation of mass and write simple reaction equations.

    1. How to recognise a reaction Colour, gas, heat or light can hint at a chemical reaction. Only a new substance is certain.
    2. Reaction or change of state? In a change of state the substance stays the same; in a chemical reaction a new one is formed.
    3. Mass stays the same In a reaction the total mass stays the same. A balance experiment shows it, and atoms explain it.
    4. Reaction equations A reaction equation shows which substances react. There must be the same number of atoms on both sides.
