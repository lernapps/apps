A copy in a second place

backup

A copy of your files in another place, for example on a stick or in the cloud. The original stays where it is.

Mia's folder tree again, but the root is called “Copy on the stick”: the same folders School, Photos and Music with the files from last Sunday. What Mia wrote after that is missing.

This is what Mia's stick looked like after her backup last Sunday. A backup is the same folder tree again, in a second place, as it was on the day of the backup. To make one, you copy the files. If you move them, you only have one file again afterwards. The BSI, Germany's federal office for information security, also advises:

  • back up regularly, for example every Sunday, or let a program do it,
  • keep the backup in a separate place, not next to the tablet,
  • check now and then that the copies open.

Saved versions

If you want to keep old versions, save under a new name: speech-1.txt, speech-2.txt. If something goes wrong in the new version, you still have the old one.

Example

Mia's backup on Sunday

  1. Mia plugs in the stick.
  2. She copies the folders School, Photos and Music onto the stick.
  3. She opens a file on the stick to check that the copy works.
  4. She unplugs the stick and puts it in her desk drawer.

On top of that, a program backs up all three folders to the cloud every night. So her files are in two places, and one of them is not at home.

A backup also helps against ransomware. Programs like that lock your data and ask for money. With a copy that is not plugged into the device, you get your data back without paying a ransom. More from the BSI (in German)
