How machines learn from data

Every day the animal shelter gets pictures of animals that were found. Jona, Selin and Mia, all aged 11, from class 6b want to build a machine that says: dog or cat? You find artificial intelligence in your everyday life, sort animal cards with a rule and train the machine with the training bag.

Contents

  1. AI in everyday life: learning from examples

    You find AI in your everyday life, look for a rule in animal cards yourself and fill a machine's training bag.

    1. What you need here (recap) A short recap: information and data, interpreting data in context.
    2. AI in everyday life Spotting artificial intelligence in everyday life: voice assistants, image recognition, recommendations and translators.
    3. Finding a rule from examples The card game with the training bag: dog or cat? Find a rule from examples and test it on new cards.
    4. Training the machine The training bag: the machine looks for the most similar example and gives its label.

    The shelter's machine keeps making the same mistake. You look for the fault in the training data, not in the program.

    1. Skewed training data One-sided training data leads to the same mistakes again and again: finding the fault in the data.

An AI tutor is an AI assistant of your choice that accompanies you while you learn with this app. It explains the topic with the examples of the pages, gives you tasks from the app and helps you when you are stuck. You solve the tasks in the app: it checks your answers.

Choose your assistant here. It is then ready under the title of every page, and every exercise has the button “Help”. You can also switch it on under the title of a page.

You use your own account with the provider. The assistant is not offered by lernapps.net: lernapps only provides the content and the exercises.

Your choice stays on this device; only your click opens the assistant. Ask your parents or teacher whether you may use it.
