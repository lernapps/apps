Bias: the fault is in the data

The program counts correctly, but the bag is one-sided: cats only small, dogs only big. The machine takes on this pattern and makes the same mistake again and again: small dogs with a short snout become cats.

With Otto in the bag, the machine is only unsure about Krümel. It only gets Krümel right if the bag holds a dog that looks just like Krümel: small, pointed ears, short snout. The shelter has no such card yet.

bias

The training data shows the world in a one-sided, skewed way. The system takes on a pattern that only holds in the data.

Remember

If a learning system keeps making the same mistake, look at the training data first. Which examples are missing, and which were chosen one-sidedly?

In real systems too

In 2018, Joy Buolamwini and Timnit Gebru studied three commercial programs that work out a person's gender from a photo. In two widely used collections of face photos for testing such programs, 79.6% and 86.2% of the people had lighter skin. So they tested with their own balanced collection. The error rate was up to 34.7% for women with darker skin (about one photo in three for the worst program), and at most 0.8% for men with lighter skin. The study does not name their training data. (Gender Shades, 2018)
