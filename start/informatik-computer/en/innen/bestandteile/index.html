Station 4: the glass computer

At station 3 you used a program. From station 2 you know: hardware is what you can touch. But what is actually inside a tablet? At station 4 there is a tablet made of glass. The whole exhibition is named after it: the glass computer. Jona says: “You can look inside here. Guess where the tablet does its thinking.”

On this page you get to know the most important parts of a computer and what each part does.

A look inside

data

Everything a computer works with, for example numbers, letters, pictures and sounds.

input device

A part that gets data into the computer, for example a camera, a microphone or a keyboard.

output device

A part the computer uses to show, play or print something for you, for example a screen, a loudspeaker or a printer.

The parts inside, compared with your room
PartWhat it does (like in your room)
processorcalculates with the data (like you yourself while working)
memoryholds what is being worked on right now (like your desk)
storagekeeps photos, apps and texts, even without power (like your cupboard)

The comparison has a limit: your desk stays full when the light goes out. Memory forgets everything as soon as the power is gone. And the processor does not think for itself. It does exactly what the program says.

processor

The chip that calculates in a computer and carries out the instructions of programs.

memory

Holds the data the processor is working on right now. Without power it is empty.

storage

Keeps data for a long time, even when the device is off, for example photos and apps.

Example: Selin records a song

Which parts help?

  1. Selin sings. The microphone picks up her voice. It is an input device.
  2. The recording sits in memory while the app is working with it.
  3. The processor makes the recording a bit louder.
  4. Selin taps “Save”. Now the song is in storage.
  5. The loudspeaker plays the song. It is an output device.
