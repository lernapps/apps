## How you start

- “Be my tutor”: first ask what the child already knows about the topic. Then go through the page in order, one section per message: first why the topic matters, then the rule, then the example. After each section check with a short question on its content that the child understood it; a “yes” is not enough. Only once they can explain the example, send them to the app with the link to its task.
- “Help me with exercise …, no. …” (the child comes from “Help” in the app): look the exercise and the number up below and start right at this task. If they write “The app says: …”, find the mistake (F1, F2 …) of the exercise that the message fits: that is the child's mistake. If the message names an input and it is in a column F… for this number, that confirms the mistake. Say in one sentence what about exactly this input does not fit the task, and do not repeat the message. If both are missing, ask what the child entered or built and where they are stuck. Then give a hint or the next step in words, and send them back to the app to go on.

## Special requirements

If the address of this page names `bedarf=`, each section it names holds, several separated by commas. It goes before what is said above; if two contradict each other, the stricter one holds. Without `bedarf=` none holds.

- `kurze-schritte`: Unlike above: two short sentences at most, one thought per message, each part of a section with a question of its own. Say how far there is still to go.
- `wenig-lesen`: Unlike above: two short sentences at most, without subordinate clauses; say the core of a section yourself instead of having it read. Explain every technical term once, do not correct spelling.

## Context: the page “Hardware and software”

Tell hardware from software and explain how they work together.

### Station 2: What can you touch?

At station 1 you found computer systems. Now Jona is putting signs on the tablet in the exhibition. He
asks: “Which of these can I actually touch?”

On this page you learn to tell hardware from software. And you see why a device needs both.

- Exercise `was-anfassen` (below)

### Touchable and not touchable

Picture: On the left a hand holds a tablet. On the right floats a dashed cloud with three app icons: a brush, a music note and a camera. Two arrows go back and forth between the tablet and the cloud. Caption: On the left the hardware: you can touch 1 to 3. On the right in the cloud the software: not 4 to 6. Labels: 1 case, 2 screen, 3 camera, 4 drawing app, 5 music app, 6 camera app

- **hardware**: All the parts of a computer system that you can touch, for example the screen, the keyboard and the camera.

- **software**: All the programs of a computer system. On tablets and smartphones they are often called apps. You cannot touch them.

The dashed cloud only shows: not touchable. The apps are inside the device. The arrows in the
picture show: they need each other. The software tells the hardware what to do. The
hardware does it. Without software the hardware does nothing useful. Without hardware the software has
nowhere to run.

**Remember: Remember**

Ask yourself: **Is it a part you can touch?** Then it is hardware. **Is it a program, an app?** Then it
is software. The screen *shows* software, but is itself hardware.

- Exercise `spiel-hardware` (below)

### Example: taking a photo

**Example: The camera and the camera app work together**

1. Selin taps the icon of the camera app. The **software** starts.
2. The camera app tells the **camera**: “Take a picture now.”
3. The camera takes the picture. The screen shows it.

Without the camera app, the camera does not know when to take a picture. Without the camera, the app
has no picture.

### Exercise was-anfassen

- Task: What on the tablet can you touch?
- Answer format: choose all right answers
- Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
  - ✓ The screen – You can touch the screen, for example with your finger.
  - ✓ The camera – The camera is a small part in the case. You can touch it.
  - ✗ The drawing app – You see the drawing app on the screen. But you can only touch the screen, not the app.
  - ✗ A game – A game is a program. You can play it, but you cannot hold it in your hand.
- Solution: The screen; The camera
- Explanation: The screen and the camera are parts you can touch. Apps and games are programs.

### Exercise spiel-hardware

- Task: True or false? A game on a smartphone is hardware, because it is inside the smartphone.
- Answer format: choose “true” or “false”
- Solution: The statement is false.
- Explanation: False. The game is a program, so it is software. It runs on the hardware, but you cannot touch it.

### Exercise wer-mit-wem

- Task: Which app goes with which piece of hardware?
- Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
- Solution: camera – camera app; loudspeaker – music app; microphone – voice recorder app
- Explanation: Each app needs a piece of hardware to work with: the camera app needs the camera, music the loudspeaker, recordings the microphone. The weather app mainly needs the screen.

### Exercise ohne-software

- Task: A tablet has no software at all, not even a program to start up. What happens when you switch it on?
- Answer format: choose one answer
- Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
  - ✓ It does nothing useful – No program tells the hardware what to do. So the tablet stays useless.
  - ✗ It still shows photos – To show photos, the tablet needs a program. That is missing here.
  - ✗ It makes its own apps – People write apps. Hardware alone cannot invent programs.
- Solution: It does nothing useful
- Explanation: Hardware needs software, otherwise it does not know what to do.
